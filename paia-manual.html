Access to Information Manual

Country Manager · Last updated 10 October 2026 · Version 2026-10-10

This manual is made under section 51 of South Africa's Promotion of Access to Information Act 2 of 2000 (PAIA). It tells you what records we hold, and how to ask for them. It also describes how we process personal information, as section 51 and section 17 of the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA) require.

In short

  • To see or correct what we hold about you, you don't need this manual. Email us, as section 12 of the Privacy Policy says. It is free.
  • A farm's records in Country Manager belong to that farm. Ask the farm first (section 7).
  • For any other record, use the request form in section 9.

3. What this manual is for

It helps you to:

  • see which records we hold, and which you can have without a formal request
  • know how to ask for a record, what it may cost, and how long it takes
  • know why and how we process personal information, and who receives it
  • know what you can do if a request is refused

4. The Regulator's guide to PAIA

The Regulator publishes a guide on how to use PAIA (section 10 of PAIA), in each of South Africa's official languages and in braille. It explains the right of access, how to make a request, and the help available.

  • Read or download it on the Regulator's PAIA guidelines page.
  • Ask the Regulator for a copy: Woodmead North Office Park, 54 Maxwell Drive, Woodmead, Johannesburg, 2191; PO Box 31533, Braamfontein, Johannesburg, 2017; enquiries@inforegulator.org.za; inforegulator.org.za.
  • Or ask us. We will email you the English guide, free of charge, or arrange for you to see it at our address during office hours.

6. Records kept under other laws

We keep records as these laws require. Where a law makes a record available to the public, you can have it as that law provides.

  • South Africa: the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002 (the supplier's details that section 43 requires are in section 1 of the Terms and Conditions); the Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008; the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013; the Promotion of Access to Information Act 2 of 2000.
  • The Netherlands and the European Union: the General Data Protection Regulation (records of processing and of personal data breaches); the General Tax Act (Algemene wet inzake rijksbelastingen), under which business and sales records are kept for seven years; the Turnover Tax Act 1968 (Wet op de omzetbelasting 1968).

8. How we process personal information

The Privacy Policy sets all of this out in full. In brief:

8.1 Why we process it

  • to sign people in and keep their accounts
  • to store, show, export and back up each holding's records for its members, on the holding's instructions
  • to send the notifications people allow
  • to keep each account to one phone and one computer at a time
  • to record which terms and policy each person accepted
  • to run trials, subscriptions and promo codes
  • to keep the service secure, stop abuse and fix faults
  • to answer the people who contact us or send a report
  • to keep the records that tax law requires

8.3 Who receives it

  • the members of a holding, as far as their role allows (section 6.1 of the Privacy Policy)
  • Google, which hosts the service: Firebase Authentication, Cloud Firestore, Cloud Functions, Cloud Storage, Firebase Cloud Messaging and Google Play
  • Esri, which receives a phone's IP address when it shows the satellite map
  • MET Norway, which receives a point on each farm for the weather forecast, and nothing that names a person
  • an authority or court with the legal power to demand it

8.4 Information sent to other countries

  • Records entered in South Africa are stored in the European Union (Google's European multi-region). Section 72 of POPIA allows this, because the GDPR protects them substantially as well as POPIA does.
  • Google's sign-in service and its notification service may carry information through the United States, protected by the European Commission's standard contractual clauses and the EU–US Data Privacy Framework.
  • Map requests go from the phone to Esri, which may handle them in the United States.
  • Weather requests go from our server in the European Union to MET Norway, in the European Economic Area.

8.5 How it is protected

  • Everything sent between the app and Google's servers is encrypted, and Google encrypts what it stores.
  • Google's servers check every read and every change against the person's membership and role on the holding. These rules are tested automatically before every change to them.
  • An account can be used on one phone and one computer at a time. Deleting an account needs a fresh password or Google sign-in.
  • Access to the database outside the app is limited to us, and used only as section 6.2 of the Privacy Policy says.
  • We keep a plan for dealing with a breach, and a record of any that happen, and tell the Regulator, the Dutch supervisory authority, holdings' owners and the people affected as POPIA and the GDPR require.

9. How to ask for a record

9.1 Your own personal information

To see, correct or delete what we hold about you, email us. We check that the request comes from you, answer within one month, and don't charge (section 12 of the Privacy Policy). You may also use the form below.

9.2 Any other record

  1. Fill in Form 2, Request for Access to Record (regulation 7 of the PAIA Regulations, 2021).
  2. Say which record you want, in enough detail for us to find it, and how you want it: a copy by email, a printed copy, or to look at it.
  3. Say which right you want to exercise or protect, and why the record is needed for it (section 53 of PAIA).
  4. If you ask for someone else, include proof that you may act for them.
  5. Send it to us by email or post (section 2).

9.3 Fees

  • We don't charge for requests for your own personal information.
  • For other records, PAIA allows a request fee (currently R140) and fees for searching, preparing and copying. They are set in Annexure B of the PAIA Regulations, and the Regulator publishes them on its fees page. We tell you what is due before we start. If the search will take more than six hours, we may ask for a deposit of one third of the access fee first.

9.4 How long it takes

We decide within 30 days of receiving the request (section 56). If the request is for a large number of records, or needs a search elsewhere or consultation, we may extend that once, by up to 30 days, and tell you why (section 57).

9.5 When we may refuse

PAIA lists the grounds (sections 62 to 70). The ones most likely to matter here protect: other people's personal information (section 63), a third party's commercial information (section 64), confidential information of a third party (section 65), the safety of people and property (section 66), privileged legal records (section 67), and our own commercial information (section 68). Where a record holds information about a third party, we notify them and consider what they say before deciding (sections 71 to 73).

9.6 If you are not satisfied

A private body has no internal appeal. Within 180 days of our decision you may complain to the Regulator, using its Form 5 (section 77A), or apply to a court (section 78).
