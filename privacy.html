Privacy Policy

Country Manager · Last updated 26 November 2026 · Version 2026-11-26

This policy explains what the Country Manager app collects, why, who can see it, how long it is kept, and what you can ask us to do about it. Country Manager is used on farms in South Africa and run from the Netherlands. This policy is therefore written to meet both South Africa's Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA) and the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

In short

  • We collect what the app needs to work: your account details, the farm records you and the people you work with type in, and the problems and ideas you choose to send us.
  • We don't show adverts, or use analytics or tracking tools (the web app's security check, 3.7, only tells a person from a bot).
  • Your account details and a farm's records are never used to train artificial intelligence (AI), ours or anyone's. We never sell them on their own: if Country Manager itself is sold, they go with it, under this policy. We use a farm's records to run the service and to find and fix faults, and we test changes only on a made-up copy, made with the holding owner's agreement (section 6.2).
  • Records are stored on Google's servers in the European Union.
  • A farm that uses the HR section can keep its employees' working times, time sheets and pay there. Only the farm's owner and directors see anybody's pay.
  • Backups are locked: only a holding's owner and directors can open one. If they switch automatic backups on, our server keeps copies of the holding's records in the European Union. They are off unless someone switches them on. The owner or a director can also save an unlocked copy, which can be read with ordinary software (section 3.6).
  • Location is used only while the app is open on your screen. Where you go is never recorded.
  • Notifications are your choice. If you allow them, your phone gets a short line when a notice goes up, a job is given to you, a to-do falls due, a planned service falls due, a machine has a fault, a fuel draw needs checking, a livestock treatment is still open, a worker's treatment waits for you to approve what came off the store, advice comes in or yours is answered, time sheets wait to be signed, a holding is offered to you or your offer is answered, a holding's subscription needs you (also before a yearly plan renews, and before a read-only holding is deleted), or your account is about to be deleted for not being used. You choose which kinds you get, in the app, apart from those that always come (section 4.2).
  • A holding that has been read-only for 12 months is deleted with its records, and so is an account nobody has used for 2 years that owns no holding. Notices come first, 90, 30 and 7 days before (section 9).
  • You can delete your account in the app, or ask us to delete it.

2. Your account and your farm's records

Country Manager holds two kinds of information, and we have a different job for each.

  • Your account. These are the details you give us so you can sign in. We decide how they are used, so we are the controller for them.
  • Your farm's records. Everything recorded inside a holding belongs to that holding: fields, livestock, stock, fuel, the attendance register and so on. The business that owns the holding decides what is recorded and who may see it. That makes the holding's owner the controller (the responsible party, in POPIA's words) for those records. We store and process them for the holding as its processor (its operator, in POPIA's words). We use them only to provide the service (storing, showing, exporting, backing up, returning and deleting the records) and to find and fix faults and test changes, as section 6.2 says. We never use them to train AI, and never sell them on their own: if Country Manager itself is sold, they go with it, under the same terms. The terms we work under are in section 9 of the Terms and Conditions.

3. What we collect

3.1 Your account

  • Your email address and password. Google's Firebase Authentication handles your password, and we never see it.
  • If you sign in with Google: the name, email address and profile photo link on your Google account.
  • If you switch on two-step sign-in: that it is on, when you set it up, and the key your codes are checked against. Google's Firebase Authentication holds these. The key is shown only on your own screen while you set it up; we never receive it or your codes.
  • Your name, the role you chose when you signed up (director, manager, worker, consultant or mechanic), and your phone number if you add one.
  • When your account was created and when you last used the app. That date also decides whether an unused account is deleted (section 9).
  • Which version of the Terms and Conditions and of this Privacy Policy you accepted, and when.
  • Which cards and buttons you chose for your dashboard on each holding. Only you can see this.
  • Which of the app's short tours you have seen, closed or been offered, whether you were shown the note about them, and whether you switched tour offers off or asked to see the tours again in Account settings. Only you can see this.
  • Which kinds of notification you switched off in the app (section 4.2). Only you can see this.
  • If you open the app's HR section: that you acknowledged its notice that checking HR, and complying with labour and tax law, is the farm's responsibility; which version of the notice, when you first acknowledged it, and whether you asked not to see it again. Only you can see this.

You need to give us an email address and a name to use Country Manager. Without them we can't create your account.

3.2 The holdings you belong to

For each holding: your role, the fields, vehicles and equipment you are assigned to, any extra records you have been given access to, who invited you and when you joined. Your name and email are copied onto your membership so that the people you work with can see who you are.

3.3 Farm records

These are whatever you and other members of a holding record in it. For example:

  • farms, fields and field boundaries; crops, planting, spraying and fertiliser records; soil tests, harvests and rainfall
  • livestock: animals, mobs, calvings, movements, treatments and sales, including the name of a buyer or auctioneer
  • vehicles, equipment, services, parts and job cards
  • the fuel ledger, stock on hand, prices and costs
  • suppliers and the names, phone numbers and email addresses of the people you deal with there
  • to-do lists and notice board posts

Every record keeps the name of the person who made it, so the farm knows who recorded what. A fuel draw or a piece of field work can also name who drove or did the work, picked from the farm's staff or typed in.

3.4 Staff and attendance records

If a holding uses the HR section, it holds details of the farm's employees. Most of them don't use the app. The details are name, South African ID or passport number, employee number, phone number, occupation, the dates employment started and ended, the type of contract, the working week, a daily attendance register with notes, and any notes kept for an employee's year-end review.

These are the farm's own employment records. Section 31 of the Basic Conditions of Employment Act 75 of 1997 requires an employer to record each employee's name and occupation, the time they worked, the pay they received, and the date of birth of anyone under 18, and to keep that record for three years from its last entry. The Department of Employment and Labour's model attendance register (form BCEA 3) records, for each day, the times work started and finished, meal intervals, hours worked, overtime, and Sundays and public holidays worked.

The ID or passport number is not part of the attendance register. The farm keeps it for its other duties as an employer: declaring its employees to the Unemployment Insurance Fund (form UI-19, under the Unemployment Insurance Act 63 of 2001), issuing employees' tax certificates under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, and being able to show a farm worker's age, because Sectoral Determination 13 for the farm worker sector forbids employing anyone under 15. The app checks that a South African ID number could be a real one, and shows the number only on the employee's page and in the register the farm exports.

The register records sick leave and whether a medical certificate was handed in. That is health information: special personal information under POPIA, and a special category of data under the GDPR. The farm keeps it to meet its duties under the Basic Conditions of Employment Act. Only the holding's directors and managers can see HR records. The one exception is the names of the current employees: everyone on the holding's staff (directors, managers and workers) can see them, and nothing else about them, so they can say who drove or who did the work. Consultants and mechanics can't see them.

Working times and agreements. A holding can record each employee's contract: the days and times they work, their meal interval, whether they live on the farm, how often they are paid, and the written agreements Sectoral Determination 13 recognises (overtime, longer hours, Sunday, public holiday and night work, a public holiday exchanged for another day, and others), with the day each was signed. For an employee under 18 it records their date of birth and home address, which Sectoral Determination 13 requires. For somebody who works at night, it records the day they were told of night work's hazards and of a medical examination, which Sectoral Determination 13 also requires.

The register, day by day. For each day it keeps who recorded it and when, why a day differed (for example harvest, or sent home), whether the employee recorded it themselves, and every change made to it afterwards, with who made it, when and why. It keeps who closed each week as right and, where the farm asks for it, which director confirmed it, and the plans of extra hours a manager makes ahead.

Time sheets. If a farm has its workers sign their time sheets, each worker chooses a PIN. We keep only a salted hash of it, which nobody can read back, not even us. With each signature we keep the days as they were signed, the time, and which phone and account it was signed on, so that the signature can be relied on later. A worker may also mark a day as not right with their PIN, saying what was wrong; we keep what they said, on which phone, and the farm's answer.

Pay. If a holding works out pay in the app, it holds each employee's pay rate and its history, whether UIF is deducted, anything given to them in kind and its value, their income tax number, whether they are paid in cash or into the bank, the deductions the farm enters (with what each is for, and the day the employee agreed to it in writing), and the payslips made final, each kept exactly as it was paid, with the tax year so far and the leave left on its last day. Only the holding's owner and directors can see pay records. Managers keep the register and never see a wage: they see only the last day a final pay run paid each person, so that a paid day goes to a director to change. A holding may also record when an employee was given their written particulars and their certificate of service.

Special personal information in pay. What a deduction is for can reveal special personal information: union subscriptions show trade union membership, and medical fees the farm paid show something about health. The farm records them because the law has it make those deductions (the Labour Relations Act s13 for union subscriptions, the Basic Conditions of Employment Act s34 for deductions generally), which POPIA allows for special personal information needed to meet an obligation in law (section 27(1)(b)), as the GDPR allows it in the field of employment law (article 9(2)(b)). Only the owner and directors see them.

A holding can keep notes through the year for each employee's year-end review: what happened and on what day, such as good work or a concern, with the name of whoever wrote the note. These are more restricted than the rest of the HR records. Only people above the employee can read them: the holding's owner can read the notes on everyone, directors the notes on managers and other staff, and managers the notes on staff who are not managers. Nobody can read the notes about themselves. Only the person who wrote a note can change or delete it. The notes are part of the farm's employment records, and an employee can ask the farm what it keeps about them (see section 12).

3.5 Subscription purchases

If you buy a subscription, Google Play takes the payment. We receive a record of the purchase so we can tell which holdings are paid for. It shows the plan, the order number, a purchase token from Google, the dates, and whether it is active, cancelled or refunded. We never see your card details. If you ask to end a yearly plan early, or a refund is owed, we keep your request, our answer and the refund (section 9). A refund goes through Google Play, or by bank transfer if Google Play can't; only then do we receive the bank details you give us for it.

3.6 Files you choose

When you import a spreadsheet, the app reads only the file you pick. When you export a spreadsheet or a backup, the file is made on your phone or computer and goes wherever you send it. After that it is out of our hands. A backup file is locked (see section 11): only the owner or a director of the holding it came from can open it, through the app. Backups saved by older versions of the app are not locked: anybody who has one can read it. Once a holding is deleted, only whoever owned it when a backup was taken can open that backup. Directors and others who will want the records after that should save the unlocked copy or the exports first.

A holding's owner or a director can also save an unlocked copy: a zip file of JSON files holding all the holding's records that the person saving it can see. It can be read with ordinary software. It is not locked. Anybody who has the file can read it, ID numbers and pay included, so keep it safe.

Payslips, the wages register, the figures for the farm's accountant (among them, for the Employment Tax Incentive, employees' dates of birth and ID numbers), the register for a payroll bureau, a crew sheet and the documents made for an employee (their written particulars and certificate of service) are made on the phone or computer as PDF files, spreadsheets or a zip, and go wherever they are sent. Those that hold pay or ID numbers say “Confidential” on them.

3.7 What your phone or browser sends

To work at all, the services the app uses receive some technical information: your IP address, the app version, and the tokens that keep you signed in. We don't use analytics, advertising or crash-reporting tools. We don't build a profile of how you use the app.

  • App Check. The app proves to our servers that it is the real Country Manager app, through Google's Firebase App Check. On Android it uses Google Play Integrity, which tells Google about the phone and the app on it. In a web browser it uses Google's reCAPTCHA Enterprise, which stores a cookie and reads information about the browser and device, to tell a person from a bot. It is a security check, not tracking. Both work under Google's terms.
  • The web app. Its files come from Google's Firebase Hosting. The code that connects it to our servers (Google's Firebase SDK), and its fonts, come from gstatic.com, which Google serves under its own privacy policy. Each of them receives your IP address.
  • These pages. The Privacy Policy, the Terms and Conditions and our other legal pages are on GitHub Pages. GitHub receives your IP address when you open one, also when you open it from the app.

3.8 What stays on your phone or computer

On a phone, the app keeps a copy of the records you open, so it keeps working without signal. It also remembers some settings, such as how the map opens. Signing out does not remove the copy of the records. It stays until the app is uninstalled or its storage is cleared in Android's settings.

The app keeps this copy and its settings out of Android's own backup to your Google account, and out of copying from an old phone to a new one. Versions before 3.7.0 did not: if your phone backed up apps to your Google account, that backup may hold the copy of the records you had opened, kept under your own Google account until Google deletes it. Version 3.7.0 and later never read such a backup back.

In a web browser, the app keeps a copy of the records you open in the browser's storage while you are signed in, and asks the browser to keep it. The copy is cleared when you sign out (the page loads again), and at the latest the next time the app starts with nobody signed in. Some settings, such as how the map opens, and the lots written down in the auction calculator stay in the browser until its data for this site is cleared. The browser also keeps the app's own files, so that it can start without signal; they hold no records. On a computer that other people use, sign out when you are done.

On the phone or computer of a holding's owner or a director, the copy includes the pay records they have opened.

3.9 Your phone, if you allow notifications

A token from Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging that lets our server reach the app on your phone, with your account's id and when it was last registered. See section 4.2.

3.10 Which phone you're signed in on

An account can be used on one phone and one computer at a time. When you sign in on a second phone, the app tells you which phone has your account and asks whether to move it; if you say yes, the other phone is signed out. For that, we keep for your phone and your computer: a phone's make and model as Android reports them, such as “Samsung SM-A145F”, and for a computer only the word “Computer”; an id the app makes up when it is installed, or first opened in a browser; when it was signed in; and when the app was last opened on it, to within an hour. Only you can see this, when you sign in somewhere else. Signing out removes it.

3.11 Problems and ideas you send

When you use “Report a problem or idea”, we receive what you write, the screenshots you choose to add, your name and email address, the app version, whether it is a phone or a computer and its make and model, and the holding you had open and your role on it. If the app has had to put the screen right after the phone's keyboard, the report also carries the app's own note of each time: when, which screen, whether a keyboard was showing and how much room it was given, the phone's make and model, its Android version and its keyboard app. The note stays on your phone until a report carries it, and the form lists it before you send. Only we read it, to fix the app or weigh the idea. Sending one is up to you.

3.12 Automatic backups

A holding's owner or a director can switch automatic backups on, daily, weekly or monthly. They are off unless someone does. When they are on, our server takes a copy of the holding's records at about two in the morning, South African time. The copy holds what a director of the holding can see. It leaves out to-dos and notices meant only for particular people, and review notes about directors. Each copy is locked like a backup you save (see section 11). Only the holding's owner and directors can see the copies, put one back or download one, and they can delete the copies kept at any time. Our server records who opened which holding's backup, and when. How long the copies are kept is in section 9.

3.13 Rain shared with the neighbourhood

A holding's owner or a director can switch on sharing its rain with the neighbourhood. It is off unless someone does, and the app says what goes out before it is switched on. When it is on, our server keeps a copy of where each of the holding's rain gauges stands and what it caught, day by day, for the current and the previous rain year. Every signed-in user of Country Manager whose own holding shares its rain can see that copy where it falls within 5, 10 or 20 km of their fields and gauges, the distance their holding chose. The copy carries no name: not the holding's, not the gauge's, not the name of the person who wrote the reading down, and none of the notes. A gauge on a known farm can still tell a neighbour whose it is. Switching it off removes the copy. The figures are as the neighbour entered them; we do not check them. We do this on the holding's instruction, as section 2 describes our role for a holding's records.

4. Location and notifications

4.1 Location

  • The app uses your phone's precise location (GPS) only while the app is open on your screen. It uses it for two things: to show where you are on the map, and to draw a field's boundary while you walk it.
  • It never uses your location in the background. It doesn't ask for background location.
  • Where you have been is not recorded or sent to us. The only positions saved are ones you choose to keep as part of the farm's records, such as a field boundary or the position of a water point.
  • You can refuse location, or turn it off later in Android's settings. The rest of the app still works.

The satellite map comes from Esri. To show it, your phone downloads map images from Esri's servers. Esri therefore receives your IP address and the part of the map you are looking at, as any website you visit would.

4.2 Notifications

  • Only if you allow them. Android asks you first. Until you say yes, the app makes no notification token for your phone, and nothing about your phone is sent to us for notifications. You can turn them off later, all of them or one kind at a time, in Android's settings. The app's Account screen takes you there.
  • Choosing which kinds. You can also choose in the app which kinds reach your phones (More → Account Settings → Choose your alerts). Your choice is kept on your account, so every phone you use hears the same. Only you can see it, and it is deleted with your account. Some always come, whatever you choose: an offer of a holding or the answer to yours, a holding's subscription notices (including the reminder before a yearly plan renews and the notices before a read-only holding is deleted), the notices before an unused account is deleted, and a notice its writer marked as important.
  • What we keep: the token that lets our server reach the app on your phone, with your account's id and when it was last registered. There is one for each phone. When somebody else signs in on the same phone, it becomes theirs, and signing out removes it.
  • What a notification says: which holding it is about, and one line. That is who put up a notice and what it says, the name of a job given to you or falling due, or the title of a piece of advice and who wrote it. It may also be a planned service on a vehicle that falls due or is still open, with the vehicle and the work; a fault reported on a machine, or left open, with the machine, how urgent it is and who reported it; a fuel draw whose reading or litres can't be right, with who recorded it, the vehicle, the litres and what is wrong; a livestock treatment you are to be reminded of that is still open in the morning, with its name and the mob or the animals' tags; or, for an owner, director or manager, a treatment a worker gave of a product kept in the store, waiting for them to approve what came off it, with the worker's name, the treatment and product, how many animals and the mob. You are only ever sent a notification about a record you can already see in the app.
  • Also: an adviser hears the farm's answer to their advice (accepted, declined or marked done, with the farm's note). For a holding's subscription, its owner and directors get a daily reminder while its free time runs out, while nobody is paying for it, and for its first 7 days read-only, and its owner hears once the day subscriptions start. Before a yearly plan renews, its owner gets a reminder, 40 to 80 business days before the year ends, with the renewal date, the price after renewal when Google Play has told us it (otherwise where Google Play shows it), and the choice to cancel in Google Play or to end the plan early (Terms and Conditions, section 6.11). Before a holding that has been read-only for 12 months is deleted, its owner and directors get a notice 90, 30 and 7 days before. Before an account nobody has used for 2 years is deleted, the person whose account it is gets a notice at the same times, if their phone can still be reached. When a holding is handed over, the person it is offered to hears of the offer, and the owner who offered it hears their answer.
  • Reminders. If a job has a due date or a reminder time, our server keeps when it should remind you until the reminder is sent, or the job is done or deleted.
  • Time sheets. If a farm has its workers sign their time sheets, its managers and directors get a reminder, on the day the farm chooses, of how many are waiting to be signed.
  • Your lock screen. Android shows notifications on the lock screen unless you change that in its settings, so anyone who picks up the phone may read them there.

Pay records, payslips, written particulars and certificates of service: only the holding's owner and directors. A worker with the app sees their own time sheet, and nothing else of the HR records.

6.2 Us

As the developer, we can reach the database through our own sign-in (the Firebase command-line tools). We look at a holding's real records only:

  • when its owner or a member asks us for help
  • to find and fix a fault that was reported to us or that our checks found
  • when the law requires it

Checks we run over the records report what is wrong, not who it is about. We also count how many people and holdings use the app, as numbers only.

We test changes to the app only on a made-up copy of a holding's records, which may also help us find faults. We make one only with the holding owner's agreement. In the copy, every name, ID or passport number, employee number and date of birth is replaced by a made-up one; phone numbers, addresses, notes, typed reasons and all pay are left out. The copy is still the holding's information, and we keep it for that holding, as its processor, in a holding that only our own account belongs to, in the same database with the same protection. It is used for nothing else, is never exported or downloaded, and is never shown outside (in a video, a published screenshot or the app's store listing). It is deleted when it is no longer needed, when the holding it came from is deleted, or when the owner withdraws their agreement.

Your account details and a holding's records are never used to train AI, ours or anyone's. They are never shown to another holding, except what the holding itself shares (its rain, see section 3.13). We never sell them on their own: if Country Manager itself is sold, they go with it, under this policy (section 6.3).

8. Where it is stored

The database is Google Cloud Firestore, in Google's European multi-region, which uses data centres in the European Union. Information entered on a phone in South Africa is therefore sent to the European Union.   • Out of South Africa. Section 72 of POPIA allows this when whoever receives the information is bound, by law or by a binding agreement, to protect it substantially as well as POPIA does. The GDPR meets that test.
  • Back to South Africa. When a holding's members open its records in South Africa, we send them from the European Union under the European Commission's standard contractual clauses (Module Four), which are part of section 9.3 of the Terms and Conditions.
  • Out of the European Union. Google's sign-in service may process account details in the United States. Google protects such transfers with the European Commission's standard contractual clauses and the EU–US Data Privacy Framework.
  • Map requests go from your phone straight to Esri, which may handle them in the United States under its own privacy statement.
  • Weather forecasts are asked for by our server in the European Union, from MET Norway in Norway. Norway is in the European Economic Area, where the GDPR applies.
  • Notifications are worked out in the European Union, then delivered by Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging, which may carry them through servers outside it. Google protects such transfers with the same standard contractual clauses and the EU–US Data Privacy Framework.
  • The web app and App Check. Google may handle the web app's files, from Firebase Hosting, and App Check (3.7) outside the European Union. Google protects such transfers with the same standard contractual clauses and the EU–US Data Privacy Framework. The code and fonts the web app loads from gstatic.com come under Google's own privacy policy and the EU–US Data Privacy Framework.

9. How long we keep it

  • Your account: until you delete it, or until nobody has used it for 2 years while it owns no holding: then it is deleted, after notices (see section 10). No time before this rule was first published counts towards the 2 years, or towards the 12 months below.
  • Farm records: for as long as the holding exists: until its owner deletes it, or until it is deleted after 12 months read-only, after notices (below). Deleting a holding deletes all of its records and the notes of which of them changed, and its automatic backups 30 days later.
  • Automatic backups: the newest 14 daily, 8 weekly or 12 monthly copies, as the holding chooses; each new one replaces the oldest. Switched off, the copies already kept stay until the holding's owner or a director deletes them, or the holding is deleted. When a holding is deleted, its copies are kept for 30 days, so that whoever owned it can bring it back, and are then deleted. Whoever owned it can delete them sooner.
  • The database's history: seven days. So that a mistake can be put right, the database keeps its own history: a record that is changed or deleted can be read as it was for up to seven days, by us only, and is then gone from it. Nobody using the app can see that history.
  • Notes of which records changed: 45 days, then deleted. A note names a record by its number and says when it changed, never what is in it.
  • Who opened a backup: 30 days, in our server's logs.
  • A holding nobody is paying for becomes read-only (section 6.7 of the Terms and Conditions). If it is still read-only 12 months later, it is deleted with all its records. Its owner and directors are told 90, 30 and 7 days before, by email, by a notification and by a banner in the app. Each notice reminds them to take a backup, the unlocked copy (section 3.6) or the exports. The first also says that our earlier promise, that a holding would never be deleted for not being paid for, is withdrawn. Subscribing, redeeming a promo code, or a director taking the holding over stops it. Taking a copy does not. Records not taken out by the day it is deleted are gone (section 7 of the Terms and Conditions).
  • Emails we send before a deletion: three years after the deletion, to show that they were sent.
  • Records you made on a holding stay with that holding, with your name on them, after you leave it or delete your account. They are the farm's records, not yours alone.
  • Former employees: the app archives employee records rather than deleting them. Section 31 of the Basic Conditions of Employment Act requires an employer to keep them for three years after the last entry. The farm decides when to remove them.
  • Pay records are tax records, which the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 has the employer keep for five years; the other employment records, three years under the Basic Conditions of Employment Act. We keep them while the holding exists. Before a holding with employment records is deleted, its owner is asked to take them first, because deleting the holding deletes them: by the app when the owner deletes it, and in the notices before a holding read-only for 12 months is deleted.
  • Time-sheet signatures, and the days a worker marked as not right, are kept with the register, and cannot be changed or deleted in the app: they are the worker's word. A worker's PIN is kept until it is reset or the holding is deleted.
  • Purchase records: seven years, as Dutch tax law requires.
  • Requests to end a yearly plan early, and refunds: seven years, with our books.
  • Your phone's notification token: until you sign out on that phone or delete your account, or until Google tells us it no longer works, for example because the app was uninstalled.
  • Which phone and computer you're signed in on: until you sign out there, another phone or computer takes your account over, or you delete your account.
  • Reminders waiting to be sent: until they are sent, or the job is done, moved or deleted.
  • Emails you send us: for as long as we need them to deal with what you asked, then deleted.
  • Problems and ideas you send: until we no longer need them, and in any case they are deleted, with their screenshots, when you delete your account.

Anything deleted is removed from Google's systems in stages: at once from the database you use, from its history within seven days (above), and from the rest, backups included, as soon as Google reasonably can and within 180 days at most, as Google's terms for Firebase require.

11. How we protect it

  • Everything sent between the app and Google's servers is encrypted, and Google encrypts the information it stores.
  • Google's servers check every read and every change against the person's membership and role on the holding. A tampered-with copy of the app still cannot open another holding's records.
  • Backups are locked. Each backup, whether you save it or our server takes it, is encrypted with a key of its own. That key is locked in turn by a key that never leaves Google's Key Management Service and that only our server can use. The server unlocks a backup only for the owner or a director of the holding it came from, so a lost or forwarded copy gives nothing away, and someone taken off a holding can no longer open the backups they took. A backup that has been changed will not open. Once a holding is deleted, only whoever owned it when a backup was taken can open that backup, so directors and others who will want the records should save the unlocked copy or the exports before then.
  • Invite codes can be made to expire and can be withdrawn.
  • Time-sheet PINs are stored only as salted hashes, checked on our server and never sent back to any phone. Pay records can be read only by the holding's owner and directors; the database refuses everybody else.
  • Only you can read your phone's notification token. Our server reads it only to send you notifications.
  • An account can be used on one phone and one computer at a time. If your account is ever signed in somewhere else, the phone you were using is signed out and tells you where, so you can change your password if it wasn't you.
  • You can switch on two-step sign-in in Account Settings. Signing in then also asks for a code from an authenticator app, so a password that has leaked is not enough on its own. Switching it off asks you to confirm it is you, then for a code. If you sign in with Google, confirming on your own phone asks for no password, so keep your phone locked.
  • Deleting an account requires a fresh password or Google sign-in, and the code as well if two-step sign-in is on.
  • The app proves to our servers that it is the real Country Manager app (App Check, see section 3.7).
  • Your phone, or the browser you use, holds a copy of the records you open (see section 3.8). Use a screen lock, and sign out of a browser that other people use. Anyone holding your unlocked phone, or using a browser you are signed in on, can see what you can see.

If we believe someone has got hold of personal information without permission, we will tell the supervisory authorities and the people affected as the law requires: under the GDPR within 72 hours where it applies, and under section 22 of POPIA as soon as reasonably possible. For farm records, we tell the holding's owner, who is responsible for telling their staff.

A farm's records may include employees aged 15 to 17: Sectoral Determination 13 allows them to work, with limits, and requires the farm to keep a record of each one's name, date of birth and address. The farm keeps these records because the law requires it, which section 35(1)(b) of POPIA allows (and, under the GDPR, to meet a legal obligation and in the field of employment law). We process them only on the farm's instructions, as for its other records.
